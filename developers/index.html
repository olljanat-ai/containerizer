    Developers and ownership¶

    Mapping hundreds of Git repositories to products and business units by hand is slow, and the repositories rarely say who owns them. The people who commit to them usually do. Admin → Developers (administrators) lists everyone who committed to the organizations' repositories in the last six months, and uses the business units you give them to suggest where the repositories and products nobody mapped belong.

    Where the list comes from¶

    At the end of every organization scan run, full or Discover, the worker reads the commit authors of the last six months from the provider's API (Azure DevOps, GitLab, GitHub), for every repository of the scan with a commit in that time. Nothing extra is cloned. It keeps:

    • one developer per e-mail address (lower case), with the name of their latest commit;
    • the repositories each developer committed to, the number of their commits there in the six months, and their latest commit there. That date only moves forward; someone whose commits are older than six months stays listed with 0 commits.

    A repository is read again when it has a newer commit than the last time, or once a week (the counts age), so most runs ask the API about few repositories. Archived and empty repositories are left out. A disabled one (itself or its project, or a new repository that starts disabled) is read only while its latest commit is in the six months, so the page shows whom to ask before enabling it, and a deprecated one nobody touches costs nothing. Automation is left out as it is read ([bot] accounts, noreply addresses, build services without an e-mail address); tick Ignore for any other service account. Why reading a repository failed is in OrgRepository.HistoryError.

    Map developers to business units¶

    Pick each developer's Business unit and press Save changes (once, for every row you changed). Suggested is the business unit of the products of the repositories they commit to, weighed by commits: it says matches when it is the one set, differs when it is another. Apply suggestions sets it for every developer without a business unit whose suggestion is at least 60% sure. Filter by No business unit or Ignored, or search by name, e-mail, repository or business unit.

    So start from what is mapped already: map a few products to business units, apply the developers' suggestions, check them, then turn to the repositories.

    Suggestions for what nobody mapped¶

    Suggestions weighs the business units of each repository's developers (not ignored) by their commits:

    • Products without a business unit, such as the ones made from projects (below): Set the suggested one, or Apply suggestions for all that are at least 60% sure.
    • Repositories without a business unit: their applications have no product, a product without a business unit, or the repository was not assessed at all (no application code, say). Map their applications on Admin → Applications to a product of the suggested business unit.

    One product per project¶

    As a starting point, an Azure DevOps project (a GitLab subgroup) is one product. After every run the worker:

    1. makes a product named after each project that has an assessed application and that nobody decided on yet (an existing product of that name is used instead);
    2. maps the applications of the project's repositories that nobody mapped to it. Each application is mapped automatically once only: if someone changes its mapping, that stays.

    On Projects → products change a project's product, or choose No product (a project with several products: map its applications yourself) or Automatic. The applications that were mapped automatically follow the project's product; the ones people mapped stay. Map now runs the step at once. Disabled projects are left alone, and GitHub organizations have no projects. The change history records every change the scan or people make (Via: scan for the automatic ones).

    In the database¶

    Table What
    Developer one row per e-mail address: BusinessUnitId, Ignored and Note are set by people
    DeveloperRepository developer × repository: Commits (six months), LastCommitAt
    OrgRepository.HistoryAt, HistoryCommitAt, HistoryError when its authors were read, its newest commit then, why it failed
    OrgProject.ProductId, ProductMappedAt the project's product; ProductMappedAt NULL: not decided (automatic)
    Application.ProductAutoAt when the application was mapped to its project's product automatically

    The procedure AutoMapProjects does the mapping; the scan worker's login (<schema>_writer) may run it, and may not change products or mappings otherwise.
